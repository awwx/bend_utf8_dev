Why UInt8 for bytes: it is the native byte type, makes the 0..255
restriction part of the type (the agreed deviation), has literals and
decidable equality, and every value produced by bit8_value fits it
exactly.  Why UInt32 for code points: it is exactly Bend's U32 (32-bit,
unsigned order), so u32_byte4 still splits 32 bits and encoding still
returns none above U+1FFFFF; UInt32.toBitVec gives the bit view.
Neither Lean String nor Char is used (Char is restricted to scalars).

Bits: Bend's Word stores the least significant bit first (WCon{b0, ...}).
Lean's BitVec.ofBoolListLE [b0, b1, ...] likewise takes the least
significant bit first, and BitVec.getLsbD w i is bit i counted from the
least significant bit.  These replace Bend's WCon pattern match and
construction; no shifts or masks are used.

OBSERVATIONS ON LAWS.bend (not fixed)
  - Domain change from deviation 1: Bend's universally quantified byte
    lists (begins_with_scalar_unique, maximal_subpart_*, decodes_first_*,
    decodes_exists/unique, and `rest` in utf8_decode_buffer_safe) range
    over all U32 lists, including values > 255; here they range over
    bytes only.  In particular Bend's safe law quantifies over non-byte
    continuations (no all_bytes on `rest`) while its prompt law restricts
    `rest` to bytes, an asymmetry in the original.  The Lean safe law is
    therefore weaker than Bend's, but only outside the byte domain.
  - utf8_decode_buffer_prompt quantifies `c` over all of U32; for a
    non-scalar c other than U+FFFD it is satisfied trivially.  Harmless.
  - decode_done is constrained only through utf8_decodes.
  - is_unicode_scalar's `cp >= 0` conjunct is vacuous (kept).
  - Comment at line 556 ("decodes_first implies that a result exists")
    is loosely worded; the law itself is clear.  Kept verbatim.
